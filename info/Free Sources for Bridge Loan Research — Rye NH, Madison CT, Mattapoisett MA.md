# Free Sources for Bridge Loan Research — Rye NH, Madison CT, Mattapoisett MA

Oct 10, 2026 · @Dean

## How a bridge loan shows up in free records (and how it doesn't)

There is no public field anywhere labeled "bridge loan." A bridge loan, when it is secured against a property, is recorded as an ordinary mortgage at the land-records office. You identify likely bridge loans by inference, reading recorded mortgages for a cluster of signals. Many bridge loans are also unrecorded or held privately, so they never appear in public data at all.

Signals that a recorded mortgage may be a bridge loan:

- **Short term.** The maturity or due date is 6–24 months out. Banks write 15–30 year mortgages; bridge loans are short.
- **Private or hard-money lender.** The mortgagee is an LLC or an individual rather than a bank or credit union.
- **Second mortgage soon after purchase.** A mortgage recorded shortly after a purchase deed, against a property the borrower is selling, is the classic pattern (borrowing against the old home to close on the new one).
- **No matching discharge yet.** A mortgage with no recorded discharge (satisfaction) is likely still outstanding.
- **Words in the instrument.** Some documents actually say "bridge," "interim," "swing," or "construction-to-permanent" in the title or body.

Free records let you *verify a specific property or lender* and *read individual instruments*. They do not let you filter a whole town for bridge loans — that bulk-filter capability is what paid platforms sell. Assembling a town-wide list from free sources is manual work.

## Rye, New Hampshire (Rockingham County)

New Hampshire records mortgages at the **county** level. Rye is in Rockingham County, so recorded mortgages and discharges live at the Rockingham County Registry of Deeds; assessor and GIS data are at the town/vendor level.

| Source | URL | What it gives you | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rockingham Registry of Deeds — "Ava" public search | [nhdeeds.org](https://www.nhdeeds.org/rockingham-home/) | Recorded mortgages, deeds, discharges. The core source. Search by grantor/grantee name, town = Rye, date range, document type = Mortgage. | Free index + images |
| Rockingham — searching records help | [searching-records](https://www.nhdeeds.org/rockingham-county-searching-records/) | How-to for the search tool | Free |
| Rye assessor database (Vision/VGSI) | [gis.vgsi.com/ryenh](https://gis.vgsi.com/ryenh/) | Owner, sale history, assessed value, parcel map/lot, field card. Confirm ownership and cross-reference the registry. | Free |
| Town of Rye Assessing Dept. | [town.rye.nh.us](https://www.town.rye.nh.us/assessing) | Full property record cards, contact for data | Free |
| NH statewide registry hub | [nh.gov](https://www.nh.gov/government/county-registry-deeds) | Confirms Rockingham as Rye's registry | Free |

**Workflow:** pull a target owner in VGSI to get the name and parcel, then search that name as grantor on nhdeeds.org, filter to Mortgage, and read each instrument for the signals above. Cross-check for a matching Discharge to see if it is still active. Note: the VGSI pages observed showed 2023 values, so confirm currency against the latest assessment year.

## Madison, Connecticut (town-level records)

Connecticut has no county government for records. Land records are filed with the **Town Clerk** of the town where the property sits. For Madison that is the Madison Town Clerk; mortgages and discharges are part of "Land Records." Madison's online land records go back to 1974; older records are in person only.

| Source | URL | What it gives you | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Madison Town Clerk | [madisonct.org/153/Town-Clerk](https://www.madisonct.org/153/Town-Clerk) | Official entry point; current portal link, fees, hours. Phone (203) 245-5672. | Free |
| Madison land records portal (USLandRecords) | [uslandrecords.com/ctlr](https://www.uslandrecords.com/ctlr/) | Recorded mortgages/deeds/discharges index since 1974. Guest search is typically free for the index; image viewing often \~$2/page. | Free index; paid images |
| Madison assessor / parcel & sales lookup | [madisonct.org — Assessor](https://www.madisonct.org/155/Assessor) | Owner, assessment value, sales history, by-street lists. Cross-reference ownership. | Free |
| Connecticut Town Clerks Portal | [cttownclerks.com](https://cttownclerks.com/) | Alternate statewide viewer some towns use; view/print index & images | Free index |

**Connecticut reality:** \~70 of 169 towns share platforms (CT Town Clerks Portal or USLandRecords/RecordHub); the rest are in-person. Guest access almost always covers the **index** for free; **images** usually cost \~$1–$2/page and certified copies require a visit. Confirm Madison's exact portal and fees on the Town Clerk page before relying on a third-party directory.

## Mattapoisett, Massachusetts (Plymouth County)

Massachusetts records mortgages at the **registry of deeds** for the county/district. Mattapoisett is in Plymouth County, so recorded mortgages and discharges are at the Plymouth County Registry of Deeds, reachable through the statewide masslandrecords.com portal. Assessor and GIS data are at the town/vendor level.

| Source | URL | What it gives you | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MassLandRecords — Plymouth registry | [masslandrecords.com](https://www.masslandrecords.com/) | Recorded mortgages, deeds, discharges. Use **advanced name search**: last + first name, town = Mattapoisett, document type = Mortgage. The core source. | Free index + images |
| Plymouth County Registry of Deeds | [plymouthdeeds.org](https://www.plymouthdeeds.org/) | Registry help, document types, book/page lookup, 17M+ images | Free |
| Mattapoisett assessor database (VGSI) | [gis.vgsi.com/mattapoisettma](https://gis.vgsi.com/mattapoisettma/) | Owner, sale history, assessed value, parcel ID. Updated yearly (January). Cross-reference ownership. | Free |
| Mattapoisett Assessors Dept. | [mattapoisett.gov/157/Assessors](https://www.mattapoisett.gov/157/Assessors) | Contact for data; assessing@mattapoisett.gov, 508-758-4100 x3 | Free |
| MassGIS statewide property map | [mass.gov interactive property map](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massachusetts-interactive-property-map) | All 351 towns; parcels + assessor data. Check the FY field for currency. | Free |

**Workflow:** confirm the owner and parcel in VGSI, then run the advanced name search on masslandrecords.com (Plymouth), filter to Mortgage, and read each instrument. The free portal images are the full recorded documents — enough to read terms, lender, and maturity. Look for a matching Discharge to judge whether a mortgage is still active.

## Cross-cutting free sources (all three towns)

These help verify lenders, add context, or catch loans tied to business entities and distress.

- **NMLS Consumer Access** ([nmlsconsumeraccess.org](https://www.nmlsconsumeraccess.org/)) — free lookup to confirm a lender or loan officer is licensed and see what states/products they operate in. Useful once you spot a lender name in a mortgage.
- **Secretary of State UCC search** (NH, CT, MA each have one) — free. For business/commercial bridge loans, secured lending against business assets shows up here rather than in land records.
- **State business entity search** (NH, CT, MA) — free. Resolve an LLC mortgagee to its principals; many private bridge lenders are small LLCs.
- **PACER** ([pacer.uscourts.gov](https://pacer.uscourts.gov/)) — small per-page fee. Loans surfacing in foreclosure or bankruptcy filings.
- **CFPB** ([consumerfinance.gov](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/)) — free consumer guidance on how bridge loans work, risks, and cost.
- **Scotsman Guide** and **PrivateLenderLink** ([privatelenderlink.com](https://privatelenderlink.com/)) — free to browse directories of bridge / hard-money lenders active in each state; good for learning *who* the likely lenders are, so you recognize them in records.
- **FFIEC HMDA data** ([ffiec.cfpb.gov/data-browser](https://ffiec.cfpb.gov/data-browser/)) — free bulk mortgage origination data by geography. Does not name borrowers or tag bridge loans, but shows lending patterns and lenders by county/tract.

## Documents to download/analyze and scraping targets

**Documents to pull and read (the actual evidence):**

- **Mortgage / deed of trust instruments** (PDF images from the registry/portal) — read the maturity date, lender name, loan amount, and any "bridge/interim/swing" language.
- **Discharge / satisfaction of mortgage** — tells you the loan is paid off (i.e., *not* a current bridge loan). Absence suggests it is still active.
- **Assessor field / property record cards** (PDF) — confirm owner, parcel ID, sale date/price to anchor a registry search.
- **Assignment of mortgage** — shows the loan changed hands; private-lender assignees are a hard-money signal.

**Scraping / bulk-access notes (check each site's terms first):**

- **VGSI assessor sites** (Rye, Mattapoisett) have predictable parcel URLs (`gis.vgsi.com/<town>/Parcel.aspx?pid=<n>`) and a Sales Search page. Scriptable for owner/sales extraction; respect rate limits and robots.txt.
- **masslandrecords.com (Plymouth)** is an ASP.NET session-based search — harder to scrape; it needs session tokens and posts. Manual advanced-search + image export is usually the realistic path.
- **nhdeeds.org (Ava)** and **USLandRecords (Madison)** are also session-based portals; index results can sometimes be paged, but images are gated. Plan on manual review for the instruments.
- **MassGIS / ArcGIS REST** endpoints (e.g. the town FeatureServer) return parcel/assessment data as JSON and are the cleanest programmatic source for MA parcels.
- **HMDA data browser** offers bulk CSV downloads by county — fully scriptable, no scraping needed.

Rule of thumb: assessor + GIS data is scrape-friendly and gives you the *candidate list*; the mortgage instruments that actually prove a bridge loan are gated images you read by hand.

## Honest limits and a suggested workflow

**Limits to keep in mind:**

- No free source lets you filter a town for "bridge loans" directly — the label does not exist in public data. Everything here is inference from recorded mortgages.
- Many bridge loans are unrecorded or privately held and never appear at all.
- Free access usually means the **index** plus images you read one at a time; bulk filtering is what paid platforms (PropStream, DataTree, ATTOM) charge for.
- Instruments are scanned PDFs — manual reading, fine for a handful of properties, slow for a whole town.

**Suggested free workflow (same shape for all three towns):**

1. Build a candidate list of recent transactions from the assessor/GIS site (Rye VGSI, Mattapoisett VGSI, Madison assessor) — focus on properties that recently changed hands or are listed for sale.
2. For each candidate, search the land-records portal by owner name, filter to Mortgage, and pull instruments recorded in the last \~24 months.
3. Read each mortgage for the bridge signals (short term, private lender, second lien post-purchase, no discharge).
4. Verify any suspect lender in NMLS Consumer Access and the state entity/UCC search.
5. Record book/page, lender, amount, dates, and your confidence level in a spreadsheet.

*This document is a research starting point, not legal or title advice. Confirm every portal link and fee on the official town/registry site before relying on it; third-party directories go stale.*
